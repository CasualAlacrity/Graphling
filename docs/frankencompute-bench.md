# frankencompute bench: power, models, TTS, parallelism (2026-10-11/12)

What frankencompute costs to run, which local models are worth serving, and which local voice
to use. Measured on the box itself: Threadripper 1900X, 64 GB, RTX 4080 16 GB, Ubuntu 26.04,
Ollama 0.40.2. Wall power came from a Tapo P110M smart plug; GPU power from `nvidia-smi`.
Electricity is €0.34/kWh. The raw logs and audio clips were deleted once the decisions were
made; this page is the record.

**Decisions:**
- **Keep gemma4** (`dc35e8d9c606`) as the served model.
- **Local TTS is Kokoro, voice `bf_isabella`.**
- **Ollama runs 3 parallel slots.** Production should set `LLM_MAX_INFLIGHT=3`.

## Power

| State | Wall power |
|---|---|
| Off (plug standby) | 2 W |
| On, idle, nothing loaded | 80–81 W |
| On, idle, gemma4 loaded (`KEEP_ALIVE=-1`) | 80–81 W, the same as nothing loaded |
| Pulling models (network + disk) | ~95 W |
| gemma4 / gpt-oss:20b serving the eval | ~265 / ~290 W |
| nemotron 30B / qwen3.8 27B (part of the model in system RAM) serving the eval | ~224 / ~210 W |

**Being on costs 1.93 kWh/day, about €0.66/day or €20/month**, whatever is loaded.

Energy per ALICE turn (one eval run: classify + respond + tool calls), above idle:

| Model | Avg turn | Wh/turn above idle | €/turn |
|---|---|---|---|
| gemma4 | 2.9 s | 0.17 | €0.00006 |
| gpt-oss:20b | 3.4 s | 0.23 | €0.00008 |
| nemotron-3.5-lightning 30B | 47 s | 1.9 | €0.0006 |
| qwen3.8 27B | 114 s | 4.2 | €0.0014 |

**How these were worked out:**
- **gemma4 and gpt-oss** ran in short, bursty windows: the box idles while the Haiku judge grades
  each answer. Their figures come from the plug's hourly Wh, minus the known idle minutes in that
  hour. gpt-oss had a clean window; gemma4 shared its hour with other phases, so treat it as ±15%.
- **nemotron and qwen3.8** held steady power for over an hour each, so the plug's 5-minute
  samples give their watts directly.
- **A model that spills into system RAM draws less power but uses far more energy per turn.** The
  GPU waits on the CPU (GPU ~6–19% utilised, CPU ~47%), and each turn takes 15–40× longer.

**Compared with the hosted fallback:**
- **Per turn:** Claude Haiku 5.5 costs about $0.0002–0.0005, i.e. €0.00017–0.00043. A gemma4 turn is
  3–7× cheaper in electricity.
- **Fixed cost:** the box costs €20/month just to be on, so it breaks even at roughly 18,000–55,000
  turns/month.
- **At launch scale:** 10 pilots × ~650 turns/month (an assumption: ~50 turns/session, 3
  sessions/week) is about 6,500 turns. Haiku-only would cost ~€1–3/month, against ~€20 for the box.
- **The box's case at launch is control and capacity, not saving money.** Running it only during
  play hours (e.g. 18:00–00:00, ~€5/month) and letting the gateway fall back to Haiku for the rest
  of the day is the cheap middle ground.

## Models

Eval: all 8 cases × 5 samples (170 runs; qwen3.8 was 1 sample, 34 runs). Haiku 5.5 judged
every run. One model handled both chat and classify, with `OLLAMA_CONTEXT_LENGTH=32768`.

| Model | Passed | Tool right | Avg turn | Fit on the 4080 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **gemma4** | **150/170 (88%)** | 165/170 | **2.9 s** | Fully on the GPU, ~5.5 GB |
| gpt-oss:20b | 114/170 (67%) | 128/168 | 3.4 s | Fully on the GPU, 12 GB |
| nemotron-3.5-lightning:30b (3B active) | 126/170 (74%) | 144/163 | 46.7 s | 48% CPU / 52% GPU |
| qwen3.8:27b | 24/34 (71%) | 25/33 | 114 s | 36% CPU / 64% GPU |

- **gemma4's misses** are almost all oblique no-questions-asked (NQA) phrasings ("stuff that fell
  off a…", "I got pirated at Seraphim"). The plain trade cases all passed.
- **gpt-oss:20b ignores Ollama's `format` parameter.** Its classify/decline output came back as
  prose ("on_topic: true"), so `with_structured_output` failed and, before `e1929ce`, crashed the
  whole eval. To get a score at all, a bench-only patch routed structured output through tool
  calls (`method="function_calling"`). Serving it would need that change in the app. It's
  perfect on price lookups but often picks the wrong tool for route requests.
- **nemotron and qwen3.8 don't fit in VRAM** and are far too slow for voice, without scoring higher.
- **Not testable locally:** Kimi K3 (2.8T parameters) and the other new large models (Mistral Large
  4, DeepSeek V4.1, GLM 5.3, MiniMax M3) are Ollama-cloud only.

## TTS

The same six ALICE lines (real eval replies with station names, commodities and `aUEC` prices,
plus one longer route line), generated by:
- **ElevenLabs** `eleven_turbo_v2_5` with ALICE's current voice, timed from the Mac including the
  internet round trip.
- **Kokoro** (82M; voices `af_heart`, `af_bella`, `bf_emma`, `bf_isabella`) on frankencompute.
- **Chatterbox-Turbo** (MIT, built-in voice) on frankencompute.

The local models ran with gemma4 also loaded, as in production.

| Line | ElevenLabs | Kokoro | Chatterbox (whole clip) | Chatterbox (clause chunks, first audio) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "What ship are you flying?" | 256 ms | 60 ms | 607 ms | 694 ms |
| Price line, ~7 s of audio | 471–2,530 ms | 80 ms | 2,331 ms | 962 ms |
| Two-option price line, ~16 s | 703 ms | 120 ms | 5,338 ms | 1,266 ms |
| Route line, ~14 s | 567 ms | 110 ms | 4,595 ms | 818 ms |
| VRAM alongside gemma4 | — | 1.35 GB | 3.05 GB | 3.05 GB |

**Verdict (Jeff, by ear): Kokoro `bf_isabella` is clearly the best, especially with numbers.**
- **Chatterbox** mangles names and numbers. Version 0.1.7 has no real streaming; splitting at
  clauses gets first audio down to ~0.7–1.3 s.
- **ElevenLabs** also stumbles on large numbers (see `tools/route_ranking.py`).
- **Kokoro** synthesises a whole reply in about a tenth of a second, so the network (LAN ~ms,
  Hetzner hop ~20–50 ms) is the larger part of its latency.

**Why it matters:**
- **ElevenLabs free tier:** ~10,000 characters/month (~10 min of speech) shared across every pilot
  behind the proxy. It's non-commercial and requires attribution.
- **Paid ElevenLabs:** speech costs roughly $0.008 per 200-character reply at the listed overage
  rate, 15–40× the LLM cost of a turn.
- **On frankencompute,** Kokoro brings speech down to electricity cost.
- **Planned `/tts` proxy:** Kokoro on frankencompute as primary, ElevenLabs as the fallback when
  the box is down, character counts metered.

**Setup on frankencompute:**
- **No `python3-venv`/pip** on the box, so `uv` sits in `~/.local/bin` and provides Python 3.12.
- **Kokoro** lives in `~/tts/kokoro-env`. It needs the spaCy model `en_core_web_sm`, installed once
  with `python -m spacy download en_core_web_sm`.
- **Chatterbox**, if it's ever needed again: its watermarker (`perth`) imports `pkg_resources`, so
  pin `setuptools<81`.

## Parallel requests

`OLLAMA_NUM_PARALLEL=3` lives in a drop-in file,
`/etc/systemd/system/ollama.service.d/parallel.conf`. It raised gemma4's VRAM from 5.5 to 6.4 GB.
The load test ran 4 cases × 2 samples per simulated pilot, each pilot a separate eval process
hitting the box at the same time:

| | 1 pilot | 3 pilots at once | Time for 72 turns |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 slot (old default) | 3.1 s per turn | 6.7 s per turn | 200 s |
| 3 slots | 2.9 s per turn | 5.0 s per turn | 162 s |

- **About 25% faster per turn under load, not 3×.** Reading the ~11k-token tools + persona prompt
  is compute-bound and shares the GPU; only writing the reply batches well.
- **Free for a single pilot,** at a cost of 0.9 GB of VRAM. A total of ~7.8 GB with Kokoro leaves
  headroom on the 16 GB card.
- **Gateway setting:** `LLM_MAX_INFLIGHT=3` matches the slots. With 3 pilots sharing the box a turn
  takes ~5 s, against ~4.3 s on Haiku, so a lower cap trades money for speed.
